Research

Current directions and selected contributions

My research connects machine learning, information theory, and wireless communications. I study how mathematical structure can improve the reliability and efficiency of systems that learn, communicate, and make decisions under uncertainty.

Reliable, Safe, and Efficient AI

How can we make learned systems more dependable while keeping their computational and representational costs manageable? My current research draws on coding theory and estimation theory to understand the tradeoffs between robustness, confidence, and efficiency in neural models.

Selected contributions

  • Coding-theoretic redundancy for robust neural classification, including output coding under shared bottlenecks.
  • Estimation limits for multiclass calibration and their connection to cross-entropy training.
  • Developing methods to verify and compress implicit neural representations using low-dimensional weight subspaces.

In my advisory role with Xymphonia AI, I contribute to research on reliable and governable AI systems.

Distributed Learning and Task-Oriented Communication

When many devices observe different parts of a system, what should they communicate to support a shared learning or inference task? I study how to design communication around the information a task needs, particularly when a receiver needs a function or collective decision rather than every individual report.

Featured project: TERRA

TERRA applies this idea to regional fire detection. Devices send compact, task-relevant reports through unsourced random access, so no device identities are transmitted, and the receiver combines the recovered evidence by region. Code

Selected contributions

  • Learned digital codes for over-the-air computation in federated learning.
  • Function computation over multiple access channels with hierarchical constellations.

Unsourced Random Access

How can a large population of devices send short messages efficiently when the active devices are not known in advance? In unsourced random access, also called unsourced multiple access, the receiver recovers the list of transmitted messages without identifying their senders. I study coding and inference methods that make this possible under practical constraints on synchronization, receiver architecture, and knowledge of the active population.

Selected contributions

  • Pilot-based and ODMA-based schemes for single- and multiple-antenna receivers.
  • Fully asynchronous unsourced random access, including over fading channels.
  • Exact MAP decoding when both the number of active users and the number of messages are unknown.

Earlier work on collision resolution and ZigZag decoding studied how to recover packets from overlapping transmissions.

Channels with Synchronization Errors

Insertions and deletions change the alignment between transmitted and received sequences, which makes their information-theoretic analysis difficult. I study capacity bounds and asymptotic characterizations for these channels, including models with substitution errors. This ongoing work is motivated in part by DNA storage.

Related outputs

  • Deletion/substitution channel capacity for small deletion and substitution probabilities · ITW 2025 · Published
